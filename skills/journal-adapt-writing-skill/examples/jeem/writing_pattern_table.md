# Final Writing Pattern Table: JEEM MVP (Anonymized)

This table is the final public-facing output of Phase 1 before the temporary dynamic skill is generated.

It is built from the per-paper style cards and the aggregated style profile. It describes writing actions, not paper findings.

---

## Revision Rules

| Section / task | JEEM-oriented pattern | Use in revision | Avoid |
|----------------|----------------------|-----------------|-------|
| Abstract | Start from the policy/economic design problem, then name the mechanism, method, central comparison, and bounded implication. | Make the abstract argumentative and mechanism-facing. | A broad climate-policy opening that never narrows to the economic object. |
| Introduction opening | Move quickly from a real policy or welfare problem to the specific economic mechanism. | Use the first paragraphs to establish why this decision margin matters. | Starting with a generic literature survey or broad public-interest statement. |
| Gap framing | Position the gap around a mechanism, welfare criterion, policy instrument, empirical design, or benchmark comparison. | Make clear what existing work cannot yet explain or compare. | "Few papers study X" without explaining the economic consequence. |
| Contribution paragraph | Tie each contribution to a model object, data object, welfare comparison, or policy-design margin. | Use 2-3 concrete contributions when possible. | Generic novelty claims or long contribution lists detached from the paper's actual design. |
| Literature review | Organize by mechanism and decision margin. | End each stream by stating what it leaves unresolved for this paper. | Chronological cataloging. |
| Model / method setup | Define the environment, actors, policy instruments, and welfare/empirical criteria before dense formalism. | Let readers see the economic object before notation intensifies. | Equations or notation before the policy/economic object is clear. |
| Results | Organize around comparisons: regimes, benchmarks, mechanisms, groups, or scenarios. | Explain why rankings or effects differ. | Result-only narration without mechanism interpretation. |
| Distributional interpretation | Separate incidence, recycling, welfare weights, feasibility, and behavioral/political channels. | Keep channels conceptually distinct. | Blending burden, welfare, and acceptability into one vague "distributional effect." |
| Discussion | Return to the central policy-design tradeoff and explain what the paper can and cannot imply. | Keep implications bounded by the model/empirical setup. | New claims not supported by the paper's design. |
| Language cleanup | Use technical but readable economics prose; prefer mechanism nouns and bounded verbs. | Replace empty transitions with causal or conceptual links. | AI-flavored phrases, inflated claims, and vague "important implications" sentences. |

---

## Priority Rules

| Priority | Source | Rule |
|----------|--------|------|
| P1 | Hard preservation constraints | Do not change facts, citations, equations, notation, numerical results, labels, cross-references, or author-defined terminology. |
| P2 | JEEM primary corpus | Target-journal writing patterns decide section structure, contribution framing, method exposition, and implication scope. |
| P3 | Secondary corpus | Use field/topical papers to enrich motivation and distributional interpretation when they support JEEM fit. |
| P4 | Optional static skill | Apply reusable writing rules only when corpus evidence does not decide. |
| P5 | Cleanup rules | Remove empty phrasing, generic novelty claims, unsupported policy claims, and AI-taste prose. |

---

## Human Review Checklist

Before using the generated dynamic skill, the user should check:

- Does the primary JEEM profile match the user's own reading of the target papers?
- Are secondary-corpus patterns clearly marked as supporting rather than overriding?
- Does the writing table preserve the manuscript's actual method and results?
- Are all privacy-sensitive manuscript details excluded from public examples?
