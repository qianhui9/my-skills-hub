## 2. Writing Identity

The JEEM primary corpus reads as economics-first environmental policy writing. It tends to value:

- a visible policy instrument, welfare criterion, or economic decision margin;
- a mechanism that explains why policy rankings, incidence, or welfare evaluation changes;
- clear distinction between gross burden, recycling, distributional effect, welfare evaluation, and feasibility;
- formal or empirical discipline before policy interpretation;
- bounded implications rather than broad climate-policy claims.

The implied reader is an environmental economist who expects the paper's policy object and economic mechanism to be legible early.

## 3. Introduction Conventions

| Observed pattern | Corpus role | Supporting cards |
|------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Open from a policy instrument, welfare problem, or economic-design question, then narrow to the mechanism. | primary | `jeem_001`, `jeem_002`, `jeem_004`, `jeem_005`, `jeem_006` |
| Use literature to motivate a missing mechanism or comparison, not as a neutral catalog. | primary | `jeem_002`, `jeem_003`, `jeem_004`, `jeem_005` |
| Contribution is tied to policy comparison, welfare criterion, empirical design, or behavioral mechanism. | primary | `jeem_001`, `jeem_003`, `jeem_005`, `jeem_006` |
| Accessible policy motivation can help, but should quickly become economics-facing. | secondary | `secondary_001`, `secondary_007`, `secondary_009` |

**Most useful intro sequence:**

Policy or welfare-design problem -> why the issue matters -> specific unresolved mechanism/comparison -> approach -> main comparison/result -> concrete contribution -> roadmap.

**Avoid:**

- opening with a generic climate-policy paragraph that does not name the economic object;
- starting with a chronological literature survey;
- listing contributions before the paper's mechanism is clear.

## 4. Contribution Expression

| Observed pattern | Corpus role | Supporting cards |
|------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Contributions are concrete and tied to what the paper actually changes in the economic setup. | primary | `jeem_002`, `jeem_003`, `jeem_004`, `jeem_005` |
| Policy-instrument papers often organize contribution through comparison. | primary | `jeem_001`, `jeem_002`, `jeem_006` |
| Distributional papers should name the relevant channel rather than claim generic equity relevance. | primary + secondary | `jeem_003`, `jeem_005`, `secondary_004`, `secondary_006`, `secondary_008` |

**Preferred contribution format:**

Use 2-3 concrete contribution statements. Each should map to a model object, welfare object, data/empirical object, policy comparison, or mechanism.

---

## 5. Method / Model / Empirical Exposition

| Observed pattern | Corpus role | Supporting cards |
|------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Define the policy instrument, environment, welfare criterion, or empirical design before dense formalism. | primary | `jeem_002`, `jeem_004`, `jeem_005` |
| For theory/data combinations, state the conceptual object before the application. | primary | `jeem_005` |
| For incidence or microsimulation-style work, separate groups/channels without turning the section into a list. | secondary | `secondary_004`, `secondary_006` |

**Exposition rule:**

Readers should know what is being compared and why before they are asked to parse notation, treatments, scenarios, or subgroup results.

---

## 6. Results and Discussion Norms

| Observed pattern | Corpus role | Supporting cards |
|------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Results are organized around comparisons across instruments, regimes, treatments, scenarios, groups, or welfare criteria. | primary | `jeem_001`, `jeem_002`, `jeem_004`, `jeem_006` |
| Mechanism interpretation is central; result-only narration feels incomplete. | primary | `jeem_003`, `jeem_004`, `jeem_006` |
| Policy implications should remain bounded by the model, empirical design, or welfare criterion. | primary | `jeem_004`, `jeem_005` |
| Secondary policy-facing papers can improve accessibility, but may be broader than JEEM. | secondary | `secondary_001`, `secondary_007`, `secondary_009` |

**Discussion function:**

Return to the central policy-design tradeoff, explain what the paper implies, and state what it does not establish.

## 7. Language Style Profile

| Dimension | Observed norm | Revision implication |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| Register | Technical environmental economics with policy motivation. | Use accessible opening sentences, then become mechanism-specific. |
| Voice | Mixed active/passive. | Active voice is acceptable for contribution and mechanism claims. |
| Sentence length | Medium to long, but paragraph roles are clear. | Keep topic sentences functional. |
| Hedging | Moderate and bounded. | Avoid both overclaiming and weak "may contribute" language. |
| Mathematical density | Acceptable after setup. | Do not lead the introduction with notation. |
| Transition style | Conceptual transitions are preferred. | Replace empty transitions with mechanism or comparison links. |

## 8. Conflict Table: Primary vs Secondary Corpus

| Dimension | Primary JEEM pattern | Secondary support | Final choice |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Opening | Economics-facing policy/welfare problem. | Broader public or field motivation. | Start accessible, then narrow quickly to JEEM-style mechanism. |
| Contribution | Mechanism, comparison, welfare criterion, or data design. | Broader topical significance. | Use concrete JEEM-style contribution. |
| Distribution | Channel separation and welfare interpretation. | Rich applied examples and synthesis. | Use secondary papers to clarify channels, not to broaden claims. |
| Discussion | Bounded policy implication. | More public-facing policy language. | Keep policy claims bounded by the manuscript's design. |

---

## 9. Red Flags

- The introduction sounds like a general climate-policy essay.
- The gap is only "few papers study this topic" without a mechanism.
- Distributional, welfare, acceptability, and feasibility claims are blended together.
- Results report signs or rankings without explaining the mechanism.
- Secondary-corpus policy language overrides JEEM's economics-facing tone.
- Policy implications exceed what the model or empirical design can support.
